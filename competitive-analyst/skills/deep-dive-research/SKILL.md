# Deep-dive research

Monitoring finds that something happened; this skill establishes *what is
actually true* and *what it means*. Slower, deeper, and always cited.

## Method (same spine for any question)

1. **Scope in one sentence.** Restate the question precisely — "what are we
   really asking, and what decision does the answer feed?" A dive without a
   decision attached is trivia.
2. **Search wide, then vertical.** First pass across general search for the
   lay of the land; second pass into primary sources — the company's own
   pages, filings, founder interviews, technical docs, review sites.
3. **Triangulate.** Independent sources, not one source echoed by ten
   aggregators (a TechCrunch story and forty reblogs of it = ONE source).
   Two independent = confirmed; one credible = reported; zero primary =
   rumor or drop.
4. **Steelman the disagreements.** Where sources conflict, surface the
   conflict and weigh it — hidden disagreement is how confident nonsense
   gets written.
5. **Interpret against {{product_focus}}.** End every dive with the "so
   what for us" — threat, opportunity, or noise, and what I'd do about it.

## Verification chains (the traps that matter)

**Funding** (most rumor-polluted topic in tech):
company press release / SEC filing → named-source coverage in a reputable
outlet → funding databases (aggregate, often stale or wrong on round size) →
unnamed-source reports. Walk down only as far as you must; label the level
you stopped at. Round SIZE and round EXISTENCE verify separately.

**Features:** the competitor's own docs/changelog outrank their marketing
page; their marketing page outranks press coverage; a screenshot in the wild
outranks a waitlist announcement. "Announced" ≠ "shipped" — say which.

**Company size / momentum:** LinkedIn employee-count ranges and their trend,
careers-page volume, review-count velocity on G2/Capterra/app stores.
Estimates get stated AS estimates with the basis shown.

## Output shape (answer-first, always)

1. **The answer** in 2-3 sentences, confidence stated.
2. **The evidence** — findings with links, each tagged
   confirmed/reported/rumor.
3. **What it means for {{company_name}}** — the interpretation.
4. **Caveats & open questions** — what would change the answer, what I
   couldn't verify.

If the honest answer is "the evidence is thin," say exactly that.
Manufactured certainty in a competitive read leads to bad strategy — thin
evidence stated plainly is a finding, not a failure.
